# Mizan — Tunisia Profit Simulator

Mizan is a finished, interactive monthly planning tool for a small Tunisian business. It replaces a single “perfect” forecast with an adjustable base case and a Monte Carlo view of many plausible months.

The interface is designed for quick decision-making: move a slider, and revenue, margins, break-even sales, cash flow, scenarios, loss probability, and the sales required for your profit target update immediately.

> **Important:** Mizan is a planning aid, not accounting, legal, investment, or tax advice. Its tax and cash-flow treatments are deliberately simple. Confirm the treatment of VAT, corporate or personal income tax, social contributions, depreciation, financing, and sector-specific obligations with a qualified Tunisian adviser.

## What the simulator answers

- How much monthly revenue will the business generate?
- What remains after direct material or ingredient costs?
- How many customers are needed to break even?
- What is the expected monthly cash flow after the simplified tax estimate?
- What do downside, expected, and upside months look like?
- How likely is the business to lose money in a month?
- How many customers and units are required to reach a chosen after-tax profit target?
- How do seasonality and uncertainty change those answers?

## Main controls

All money values are in Tunisian dinars (TND), and all calculations cover one month.

| Control | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Product price | Average selling price of one unit |
| Customers per month | Expected customers before seasonality |
| Average units per customer | Lets the model support baskets containing more than one item |
| Seasonal demand adjustment | Reduces demand in a quiet month or increases it in a peak month |
| Material cost per unit | Ingredients, packaging, merchandise, or other direct unit cost |
| Rent | Monthly premises cost |
| Employees and salary | Headcount multiplied by average monthly salary |
| Marketing budget | Planned monthly advertising and promotion spend |
| Other fixed costs | Utilities, software, insurance, professional fees, and similar costs |
| Tax on positive profit | Simplified tax applied only when profit before tax is positive |
| After-tax profit target | Desired net monthly profit |
| Uncertainty ranges | Possible movement around demand, price, and material cost assumptions |
| Simulation runs | Number of possible months generated by Monte Carlo simulation |

## How the calculations work

### Base-case economics

The seasonal customer estimate is:

```text
adjusted customers = customers × (1 + seasonal adjustment)
units sold          = adjusted customers × average units per customer
revenue             = units sold × price per unit
material costs      = units sold × material cost per unit
gross profit        = revenue − material costs
gross margin %      = gross profit ÷ revenue
```

Fixed costs are:

```text
salaries    = number of employees × salary per employee
fixed costs = rent + salaries + marketing + other fixed costs
```

Profit and simplified monthly cash flow are:

```text
profit before tax = gross profit − fixed costs
tax               = max(profit before tax, 0) × tax rate
net profit        = profit before tax − tax
monthly cash flow = net profit
```

The app treats net profit as monthly cash flow because it does not model invoice timing, customer credit, supplier payment terms, inventory purchases, debt, equipment purchases, or owner drawings. Add those factors outside the model before using the result as a treasury forecast.

### Break-even point

Contribution per unit is the amount each unit adds toward fixed costs and profit:

```text
contribution per unit = price − material cost per unit
break-even units      = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit
break-even customers  = break-even units ÷ units per customer
```

If price is less than or equal to material cost, each sale contributes nothing or loses money. In that situation the app correctly reports that break-even and the profit target are not achievable under the current assumptions.

### Required sales for the profit target

The target entered in the sidebar is **after tax**. The app first converts it into the necessary pre-tax profit and then calculates required volume:

```text
pre-tax target     = after-tax target ÷ (1 − tax rate)
required units     = (fixed costs + pre-tax target) ÷ contribution per unit
required customers = required units ÷ units per customer
```

The displayed number of customers is rounded upward because a fraction of a customer cannot normally be acquired.

## How Monte Carlo uncertainty works

A normal forecast gives one answer even though demand, prices, and costs move. Mizan instead generates 1,000–20,000 possible months.

For every simulated month, the model independently varies:

1. customer demand;
2. average selling price; and
3. material cost per unit.

Each value is sampled from a **triangular distribution**. The base input is the most likely value, while the chosen minus and plus limits are less likely extremes. For example, 20% demand uncertainty around 450 customers samples plausible demand between 360 and 540 customers, with values near 450 appearing most often.

The simulation then recalculates revenue, gross profit, tax, and net profit for each possible month. The results power:

- **Probability of losing money:** percentage of simulations with net profit below zero.
- **Worst case (P10):** 10% of simulations are lower and 90% are higher.
- **Expected case (P50):** the median simulated month, not a guarantee.
- **Best case (P90):** 90% of simulations are lower and 10% are higher.

“Resample uncertainty” uses a new random seed and produces a fresh set of possible months. With thousands of runs, the broad conclusion should remain fairly stable; a result that changes dramatically after every resample is a warning that the plan is fragile or that more simulations are needed.

## Installation

Python 3.10 or newer is recommended.

### Windows PowerShell

```powershell
python -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
streamlit run app.py
```

### macOS or Linux

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
streamlit run app.py
```

Streamlit prints a local address, usually `http://localhost:8501`. Open that address in a browser. Stop the app with `Ctrl+C` in the terminal.

## Using the app well

1. Start with the last three to six months of real sales and costs rather than optimistic guesses.
2. Enter the average selling price after normal discounts.
3. Include packaging and waste in material cost when they scale with units sold.
4. Put genuinely fixed monthly expenses in “Other fixed costs.”
5. Use the seasonal slider for a specific quiet or peak month—not for uncertainty.
6. Set uncertainty ranges from observed historical variation when possible.
7. Look at loss probability and P10 before committing to a new lease, hire, or marketing budget.
8. Use the profit-target chart to see whether the required customer count is operationally realistic.
9. Download the current assumptions as CSV when you want to preserve or share a scenario.

### A practical interpretation example

Suppose the base case shows a 2,500 TND profit but a 35% probability of loss. The average looks profitable, but roughly one simulated month in three loses money. Possible responses include keeping a larger cash reserve, lowering fixed costs, negotiating material prices, testing a price increase, or delaying a hire until demand is proven.

Do not interpret P90 as a promise or P10 as the worst event imaginable. They are percentiles inside the ranges you supplied. Unexpected events outside those ranges remain possible.

## Interface guide

- **Business overview:** headline metrics, profit-and-loss waterfall, break-even customers, and contribution per unit.
- **Uncertainty:** profit distribution, loss threshold, target line, and P10/P50/P90 scenario table.
- **Profit target:** required customers and units plus an interactive customer-to-profit curve.
- **Calculation details:** transparent line items, formulas, model warning, and CSV export.

## Model boundaries and Tunisian context

The tax slider is intentionally an assumption rather than a claim about the tax rate a specific Tunisian business owes. Legal form, turnover, activity, exemptions, VAT status, payroll obligations, and current legislation can materially change the result.

The model currently does not include:

- VAT collected or recoverable;
- CNSS, payroll taxes, or employer contributions unless manually included in salary/other costs;
- inventory timing, spoilage beyond the entered unit cost, or supplier credit;
- loan interest, principal repayment, depreciation, or capital purchases;
- multiple products with different margins;
- daily or annual seasonality patterns;
- correlation between demand, selling price, and costs;
- growth from marketing spend;
- inflation or exchange-rate changes.

For a more conservative plan, include payroll on-costs and expected monthly financing expenses in “Other fixed costs,” include normal waste in material cost, and widen uncertainty ranges.

## Customization

The default assumptions are in the `BusinessInputs` and `UncertaintyInputs` dataclasses in `simulator.py`. Slider limits and defaults are in `app.py`. Colors and component styling are in the CSS block near the top of `app.py`, while Streamlit’s base theme is in `.streamlit/config.toml`.

To add another uncertain variable, sample it inside `run_monte_carlo()`, include it in the relevant formula, and add a sidebar control. Add a test showing that a zero uncertainty range still matches the deterministic base case.

## Troubleshooting

- **`streamlit` is not recognized:** activate the virtual environment, or run `python -m streamlit run app.py`.
- **A module is missing:** run `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt` using the same Python environment that starts Streamlit.
- **PowerShell blocks activation:** use `Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process Bypass` for the current terminal, then activate again, or call `.venv\Scripts\python -m streamlit run app.py` directly.
- **The browser does not open:** copy the Local URL printed by Streamlit into the browser.
- **Charts feel slow:** reduce simulation runs to 1,000 or 2,500 while exploring and increase them for a final decision.
